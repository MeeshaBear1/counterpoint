## Claim

**Four tools that show you the shape of a hard conversation, and never tell you who won.**

The claim, not the category. "AI communication tools" is the category and it is worth nothing.

## Reader

Someone in the middle of it: a fight with a partner that has stopped being about the thing it
started about, a diagnosis they are reading with a phone in one hand, a contract nobody will
explain, an idea they are embarrassed to admit they do not follow. They walk in having already
met a tool that either picked a side or made something up, and they are carrying that.

They are not looking for a verdict. They are looking for the shape of the thing.

## The two modes, and where each one speaks

| Mode | Where | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Invitation** | hero, CTAs, tool cards, the router, the questions | The reader is the subject. "You". Shorter sentences. Zero proper nouns except the tool names. The offer as a wish. |
| **Credential** | the three vows, how it works, the footer note | The work is the subject. Longer sentences. The pivot on `so`. No second person. |

Never averaged. A draft that lands between the two reads as neither, and that is the failure
this table exists to prevent.

## The third register — captions on photographs (added 2026-09-18)

The two modes above each put a subject into the sentence: Invitation makes it the reader,
Credential makes it the work. A caption under a photograph of two people already has a subject,
and it is standing in the frame. Write either mode over it and the sentence competes with the
picture — *"you have had this conversation"* tells the reader what they are looking at, and
*"our tools sit inside moments like this"* sells across it.

So captions are **observation**, and that is a third register rather than a blend of the other
two. Its shape:

- One sentence, eight to twelve words, present tense. No second person, no first person.
- It says what is happening in the frame **one beat past what is visible** — the thing a viewer
  works out a second after looking. *"An hour in, and it is not about the dishes any more."*
- **No product word, ever.** Not a tool name, not *shape*, not *diverge*, not *altitude*. A
  photograph is not an advertisement, and a caption reaching for the claim turns it into one.
- No numeral, no proper noun, and no adjective left to do the work alone.

It appears in `<figcaption>` and nowhere else. A caption's register never leaks upward into a
stand, a door or a receipt, and the two modes still govern every other line on the page.

## The muted stance — and why

**The dare close is muted.** House-wide the close is *"Do you want to join us and can you keep
up?"*. HOUSE-VOICE permits a venture selling comfort, grief-care or reassurance to mute it in
its block and say so.

These tools sit inside a fight with a partner and a diagnosis somebody is frightened of.
"Can you keep up?" is the wrong sentence to say in that room, to that person, on that day. The
**modest close** stands here instead — asking for almost nothing is the most credible ask
available, and it is the only one this subject can carry.

Everything else carries: creation over the mundane, the clock as a proof location, no meeting
for its own sake, function then beauty, never a claim without a location for its proof.

## Banned, on top of C7

| Banned | Because |
|---|---|
| **"winner", "wins", "settles it", "who's right"** | The product's entire refusal. Using the word even to deny it plants it. |
| **"resolve", "resolution"** | We do not resolve a disagreement. We draw it. Promising resolution is the one lie available to this product. |
| **"therapy", "therapist", "counselling", "treatment", "heal"** | The reference is a licensed clinical service. We are four text tools and we are not qualified. This boundary is legal as well as honest. |
| **"AI-powered", "powered by AI"** | Mechanism above the fold. Zero of seventeen campaigns lead with how it works. |
| **"simply", "just", "easy"** | The conversations this serves are not easy, and saying so insults the reader on arrival. |
| **"safe space"** | A claim about the reader's feelings that no software can keep. |

## Proof, and where it lives

Numbers go on tiles, never inside sentences. The only numbers this property owns are
**structural and checkable in under a minute**:

- 4 tools, live
- 5 altitudes in Rosetta, first-timer to fellow expert
- 4 kinds of disagreement Crux tells apart
- 3 vows

**There are no usage numbers, and none will be invented.** No user counts, no satisfaction
scores, no testimonials. C9 is absolute here, and the reference's strongest device — "More than
60,000 5-star reviews" — has no honest analogue on this page. The trust device is the refusal
list instead.

**Compliance, non-negotiable:** Rosetta is not medical or legal advice. It translates what a
document says; it never says what to do about it. That sentence survives every rewrite.

## Stance

**Opinions** (arguable, on purpose):
- A tool that picks a side in your argument has made your argument worse.
- Most disagreements are not about the thing they appear to be about.
- "Nobody knows" is a better answer than a confident one that is wrong.

**Refusals:**
- Never names a winner.
- Never fills a gap with an invented fact.
- Never hides the edge of what it knows.
- Never promises resolution.

**Admission** (body only, never a hook): these tools do the small half. They show you the
shape of the thing. The hard half — deciding, conceding, apologising — was never ours to take,
and we have not built anything that can.

## Canonical — these are the voice

1. `The hardest conversations, held better` — the existing H1. Promises an outcome, argues no
   mechanism, five words. Kept because nothing written since beats it.
2. `It never picks a side. It never fakes a fact.` — the refusal pair, and the whole product
   in eleven words.
3. `Nobody wins a song.` — Counterpoint's line. Refuses scoring by describing the medium.
4. `A translation, never a verdict.` — Rosetta's line, and its compliance boundary said as a
   promise rather than as a disclaimer.
5. `The judgment comes back to you. That part was never ours to take.` — the modest close in
   its working form: names the limit, and hands the reader the thing they actually came for.
